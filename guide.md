## Getting around

### Logging in
Log in with your **username or your email**, and your password (press **Enter** or tap the button).

- **New here?** If your company already uses FleetOptima, your Admin adds you and you get an **invite** (see **Adding someone** below). If you're setting FleetOptima up for your own company, tap **New here? Start free** under the Login button. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **New here?** Your landlord adds you and you get an **invite** by email, or a QR code to scan (see **Adding someone** below). <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Forgot your password?** Tap **Forgot password?**, type your username or email, and we'll email you a link to pick a new one. The link works once and lasts an hour. Your old password keeps working until you use it, so if you didn't ask for it, just ignore the email.
- **No email on your account?** Ask your Admin to tap **Reset password** on your user. They'll get a link or a QR code for you.
- **The first time you log in**, you're asked to agree to the **Terms of Service** and **Privacy Policy** (tap either to read it). You're asked again only if they change.

### Staying logged in
Once you log in, **you stay logged in on that phone or computer**, even if you close it and come back tomorrow. You're only asked again if you haven't opened FleetOptima there in **30 days**, if you **log out**, or if your password changes.

- **Log out** (account menu) ends it on that device only.
- **Sign out everywhere** (Account settings) ends it on every phone and computer at once, including the one in your hand.
- **Changing your password** logs you out everywhere else, but not where you changed it.

*Why:* a mechanic with greasy hands shouldn't have to type a password every morning, and a lost phone shouldn't stay logged in forever. Sign out everywhere fixes that in one tap. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* nobody wants to type a password every time the sink leaks, and a lost phone shouldn't stay logged in forever. Sign out everywhere fixes that in one tap. <!-- for: landlord -->

**The first load can take up to a minute.** When nobody has used FleetOptima for a while, the server rests, and the first visit wakes it up. You'll see **"The server is waking up, hang tight."** That's normal; after that it's quick.

Tap **urgent** or **open jobs** to see just those work orders, or the maintenance count to see that list. With nothing waiting, it says **All caught up**. The first time you open FleetOptima each day it's a big card; after that it shrinks to one slim line so it's out of your way.

Below it, Home is grouped into sections:
- **Quick actions**: the everyday jobs, as wide buttons on top. Right now that's **New Work Order** (and **Update miles** if you run a vehicle). <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Quick actions**: **New Maintenance Request**, as a wide button on top. <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Maintenance**: what's **due** (red) or **coming up** (amber), most urgent first, if you're allowed to see maintenance. Tap a due one to open its work order, or a coming-up one to open the asset. **See all** lists every one. It's not there when nothing is due.
- **Fleet**: **Assets**, **Work Orders**, **Parts** and **Costs** (Costs only for people allowed to see prices). <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Properties**: **Properties**, **Maintenance Requests** and **Costs** (Costs only for people allowed to see prices). <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Admin**: **Users** and **Groups**. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Admin**: **Users** and **Groups**. <!-- for: landlord -->

You only see the tiles your groups let you use (see **Groups and permissions**), and only the parts your company has turned on (see **Settings for your whole company**). A tile opens that list. To add something new, open its list and tap **+ New** (bottom right).

**Nothing on Home at all?** Whoever added you hasn't given you anything to see yet (no group, or nothing assigned to you). Home says so: ask them.
A tenant's Home is short on purpose: **New Maintenance Request**, and **Maintenance Requests** to check on the ones you've sent. <!-- for: landlord -->

FleetOptima runs in your web browser, but you can **install it like an app**: it gets its own icon, opens full screen without the browser's address bar, and has its own spot when you switch between apps. It's the same FleetOptima with the same login, and it updates itself, so there's no app store and nothing to download again.

**Android (Chrome):** open FleetOptima, tap the **⋮** menu (top right), then **Add to Home screen** (or **Install app**), and confirm.

**iPhone (Safari):** open FleetOptima, tap the **Share** button, then **Add to Home Screen**.

*Why:* without the browser's bars there's more room on screen, and nothing covers the buttons at the bottom, like the camera's shutter.

### Adding, changing and deleting
- **Add:** open a list and tap **+ New** (bottom right).
- **Change:** open the thing and tap **Edit**, then **Save**.
- **Delete:** open it and tap **Delete** (on a work order it's under the **⋯** next to the job's status). The app asks first. Then it's gone from the list, and a bar at the bottom says **Deleted ... Undo** for a few seconds: tap **Undo** and it's back like nothing happened. *Why:* everyone taps the wrong thing sometimes.
- Deleting hides it everywhere, but its history is kept: a deleted asset's work orders and records stay on the books. *Why:* an asset you sold last year still had real repairs and real costs, and your totals shouldn't change because it's gone.

## Assets

An asset is one vehicle or piece of equipment: "Truck 12," "the Bobcat." <!-- for: fleet -->
A property is one place you look after: "Apt 1A," "the duplex on Elm," "the laundry room." <!-- for: landlord -->

### Adding an asset
**Home → Assets → + New.** Pick the **asset type** first: the form then shows only what that type uses. A **Vehicle** asks for a **VIN**, **Equipment** asks for a **Serial Number**, and a place (a shop, a yard) asks only for a name and an address. <!-- for: fleet -->
**Home → Properties → + New.** Pick **Property** as the type, give it a **Nickname** ("Apt 1A") and its **Address**. That's all a property needs: no make, no model, no meter. <!-- for: landlord -->

The **Address** keeps exactly what you type, spaces and all ("12 Elm St, Apt 1A"). <!-- for: landlord -->
The ID box keeps what you type, spaces and all: a serial number like "SN 4471 B" stays as written. Only a real VIN (17 characters that pass the check digit) is saved in capitals. <!-- for: fleet -->

### Naming an asset
Give it a **Nickname** and that's what it's called everywhere: lists, dropdowns, work orders.
No nickname? It shows the **last 6 characters of the VIN** ("VIN: ...123456"). Nothing at all? **Unnamed asset**, which is your hint to give it a name. <!-- for: fleet -->
No nickname? It shows as **Unnamed property**, which is your hint to give it one. <!-- for: landlord -->

*Why nicknames:* nobody in a shop says "the one ending in 4C7". They say "Truck 12." The app should talk the way you do. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why nicknames:* nobody says "12 Elm St, unit A, rear." They say "Apt 1A." The app should talk the way you do. <!-- for: landlord -->

### The asset's page
Open an asset and the **essentials** are on top: name, type, reading, and **Added by** (who put it in FleetOptima). Under them are **headers you tap to slide open**: <!-- for: fleet -->
Open a property and the **essentials** are on top: name, address, and **Added by** (who put it in FleetOptima). Under them are **headers you tap to slide open**: <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Assigned people**: who uses it (see **Who uses it** below).
- **More details**: everything else saved on it (the rest of the VIN lookup, see below). <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Maintenance**: its maintenance rules, and what's coming up or due (see **Maintenance**).
- **Work orders**: this asset's jobs, newest first. Tap one to open it.
- **Readings**: its current miles or hours, and **Log reading**. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Costs**: what it cost over the last 6 months, parts and outside services (only if you can see costs).

A header starts closed and doesn't load anything until you open it. Tap it again to close it.

*Why:* an asset collects a lot over the years. The things you check every day stay on top, and the rest is one tap away instead of a long scroll.

### Who uses it (assigned people)
**The asset → Assigned people → Change.** Search, tick everyone who uses it, and **Save**. The ones you picked show as chips (tap the **x** to take one off). It works the other way round too: **a person → Assigned assets → Change** picks every asset they use.

- **As many as you like, both ways.** A truck two operators share, an operator who drives three trucks: tick them all. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **As many as you like, both ways.** Roommates in one apartment, a maintenance person who looks after five buildings: tick them all. <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Being assigned is how people see it** when their groups don't let them see every asset (see **What people see**).
- **Who else sees it anyway** is written under the list: admins, and everyone in a group that can see every asset. On a person, **Sees every asset** says so, and why (an admin, or which group).
- Only people in your company show up to pick.

*Why both ways:* when a new hire starts, you go to them and tick their trucks; when a truck is sold, you go to it. Whichever is in front of you. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why both ways:* when a tenant moves in, you go to them and tick their apartment; when a building gets a new maintenance person, you go to it. Whichever is in front of you. <!-- for: landlord -->

<!-- for: fleet -->
### Logging a reading
**The asset → Readings → Log reading.** Type what the odometer or hour meter says now. Readings only go up, so the app stops you if the number is lower than the last one. You can also log one when you **complete a job** (the app asks). **Update miles** on Home lists every machine you're assigned to.

*Why it matters:* maintenance that runs on miles or hours (see **Maintenance**) only knows a truck is due if someone tells it the reading. Costs per mile and per hour come from readings too.

<!-- for: fleet -->
### What's on the form
The form has two parts:
- **The top is how it is right now:** nickname, asset type, the **unit** it counts in, and its **current reading**. The reading is named after the unit: **Current miles**, **Current hours** (just **Current reading** until a unit is picked).
- **Specifications are what it is:** the VIN (or serial number), make, model and year. These hardly ever change once they're filled in.

**Who runs it** isn't on the form: assign people on the asset's page after you save it (see **Who uses it**).

*Why split it:* the things you update all the time sit on top where your thumb is, and the things you set once stay out of the way.

The **unit** fills in from the asset type: a Vehicle counts **Miles**, and you can change it (a truck with an hour meter, say). Things with no meter (a trailer, a building) count in **None**. *Why units matter:* maintenance runs on them. "Every 3,000 miles" and "every 250 hours" only work if the app knows which one this machine counts. Mileage or hours can be typed or **read off a photo of the dash** (camera button; see *Reading it off a photo*).

<!-- for: fleet -->
### The VIN fills in the rest
Enter the VIN any of three ways:
- **Type or paste it.**
- **Scan the barcode** (barcode button in the field) on the VIN sticker. That sticker is usually on the front door jamb. Work vans and trucks tend to have one; many cars don't.
- **Read it off a photo** (camera button) of the VIN plate at the bottom of the windshield. See *Reading it off a photo* below.

Once there's a VIN that passes its **check digit** (a built-in math check every North American VIN has), FleetOptima looks it up with **NHTSA**, the US government's vehicle database, and fills in the make, model, year and more. **VIN settings** (Org settings) decide which of those details sit on the form; the rest go into **More details**, so nothing is thrown away. Anything you already typed is never overwritten. It's the number that decides, not the asset type: a trailer with a VIN gets looked up too.

**More details** holds everything else the VIN lookup found (engine, body style, plant...). You can edit any of it, and your edits stick: looking the VIN up again doesn't overwrite them. *Why keep it all:* you don't need engine size today, but the day you're ordering a part, it's already there.

A VIN that fails the check digit doesn't look anything up. Compare it with the plate. Serial numbers are saved as typed.

<!-- for: fleet -->
### Asset types
Every asset is one type, and the types come built in, so there's nothing to set up: <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Vehicle**: make, model, year and miles, with a VIN. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Equipment**: make, model, year and hours, with a serial number. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Tool**: small tools like saws, pole pruners, blowers and trimmers. No VIN, no meter, $1 a month instead of $8. <!-- for: fleet -->
Every property is the **Property** type, already set up for you: just a name and an address. <!-- for: landlord -->

**Need another kind?** Email us and we'll add it, usually the same day. That's also how you get one your maintenance rules can aim at, like "Bucket truck". <!-- for: fleet -->

The type only picks where a new asset starts. Any asset can count **miles**, **hours**, or anything else you track: change its measuring unit on the asset itself. <!-- for: fleet -->

## People

### Users and roles
**Home → Users** lists everyone in your company. Each person has a **role**:
| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| **User** | Whatever their **groups** allow (see **Groups and permissions** below). |
| **Admin** | Everything, whatever their groups. |

Everyone only ever sees their own company's things and people. Tap a person to see when they last logged in, and **Assigned assets** (what they use; see **Who uses it**).

*Why:* so the person reporting a flat tire can't accidentally delete a coworker's account or change company settings. Give Admin to whoever runs the shop, and groups to everyone else. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* so a tenant reporting a leak can't change anyone else's account or your settings. Give Admin to whoever runs things, and groups to everyone else. <!-- for: landlord -->

### Name and username
Everyone has both. The **Name** is what everybody sees: on lists, on work orders, in history, and in Home's hello. The **Username** is what they type to log in (they can also log in with their email). They're separate on purpose: fix a name or add a last name (**Matthew** to **Matthew Smith**) and their login stays the same. Only change the **Username** box on **Edit** if they should log in with something new, and tell them when you do.

### Adding someone
**Home → Users → + New.** Fill in their **Name**, a **Username** (what they log in with), their **Email** (optional), their **Role** (it starts on **User**, the least access, so making someone an Admin is always on purpose) and their **Group**. Then pick **How will they sign in?**

- **Send an invite** (the usual way): no password from you. After you save, an invite pops up with a **QR code** and a **Copy link** button, and if they have an email, it's emailed to them too. They open it, check their name and username (you can leave the username blank and let them pick), choose a password, and they're in. The invite works once and lasts **7 days**. Making a new one cancels the old one.
- **I'll set their password:** a password box shows. Type one and tell them yourself.

Then open them and **assign** what they use (**Assigned assets → Change**), unless their group sees everything. <!-- for: fleet -->
Then open them and **assign** their apartment (**Assigned assets → Change**). A tenant with nothing assigned can log in but has nowhere to send a request. <!-- for: landlord -->

**No email?** That's fine. Show them the QR code on your phone and they scan it with their own phone's camera, or text them the link.

**Each email can only be on one account**, and every username is unique. *Why:* so email login and Forgot password always mean exactly one person, and nobody else's inbox can open your reset link.

### When someone can't get in
Open their user and tap **Reset password**. You get a link and a QR code, same as an invite (emailed too, if you want). Their old password keeps working until they use the link, which works once and lasts **24 hours**. You can also tap **Invite** on anyone who never finished setting up.

### Lost phone, or someone left
Open their user and tap **Sign out everywhere**. It asks first, then logs them out on every phone and computer. A screen they already have open can keep working for up to **15 minutes**, then it's done. If they've left for good, **Delete** their user too. *Why:* a lost phone or a shared computer someone forgot to log out of shouldn't keep a door open.

**Moved on to another asset?** Take them off the old one (**Assigned people**). They stop seeing it and can't file new work orders on it, but they still see the ones they already reported, so they can check how those turned out. <!-- for: fleet -->
**Tenant moved out?** Take them off the apartment (**Assigned people**). They stop seeing it and can't send new requests on it, but they still see the ones they already sent, so they can check how those turned out. <!-- for: landlord -->

### Your account
**Account menu → Account settings → Your details** has your **name**, **username** (what you log in with) and **email**. Change any of them and tap **Save**. Your email is where email alerts and password links go, so if you don't have one there, add it. <!-- for: all -->
*Why you can change these yourself:* a typo in your name, a new email, a username you'd rather have. None of that should need someone else to fix it.

**Account menu → Account settings → Change password.** Type your current password, then the new one twice. Forgot the current one? There's a link to **Forgot password** right there. Changing it logs you out on your other devices.

**Sign out everywhere** (same page) logs you out on every phone and computer, this one too. Use it if you lost a phone or logged in somewhere you shouldn't stay logged in.

**Delete account** (the red button on the same page) asks if you're sure and for your password, and you can't log in again afterward. Your work orders and history stay with the company. **If you're your company's only Admin, deleting your account deletes the company too**: everyone in it loses access. FleetOptima warns you and makes you tick a box first. *Why:* a company with nobody in charge can't add people, change settings or fix anything. Make someone else an Admin first if the company should keep going without you.

**Passwords** need at least 6 characters, with an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a number and a symbol.

### Email alerts
**Account menu → App settings → Email alerts.** Your own switches; nobody else's change.
- **New Work Orders**: **Off**, **Only urgent ones**, or **All of them**. Only for people who can work on jobs. Includes the ones maintenance makes on its own.
- **Updates on what I reported**: an email when a work order you reported is **started**, **finished** or **cancelled** (not for notes).

You **never get an email about something you did yourself.** Every alert has a **turn these off** link at the bottom that works without logging in.

**No email on your account?** Then alerts have nowhere to go, and **Email alerts** says so at the top, with a link to **Account settings** to add one.

Everything starts **off** for fleets: turn on what you want. <!-- for: fleet -->
For landlords, **New Maintenance Requests** starts **on** (all of them). Tenants can turn on **Updates on what I reported** to hear when someone's on it. <!-- for: landlord -->

*Why your own switches:* the owner wants every new job; the mechanic on nights only wants the urgent ones; the operator only wants to know when their truck is fixed. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why your own switches:* the landlord wants every request; the maintenance person might only want urgent ones; a tenant only wants to know when someone's on their sink. <!-- for: landlord -->

<!-- for: fleet -->
### VIN settings
**Account menu → Org settings → VIN settings.** Tick which details from a VIN lookup sit on the asset form. The ones that are on are listed first; the rest (there are over a hundred) are under **More fields**. Anything left off still goes into **More details**, so nothing is lost. *Why:* a VIN lookup returns a lot. A plumbing company might care about towing capacity and never about airbags. Keep the form to what *your* people actually look at.

## Groups and permissions
What each person can see and do comes from their **groups**. A group is a set of things people can do, like "Mechanic" or "Operator". Put someone in a group and they can do what it allows. <!-- for: fleet -->
What each person can see and do comes from their **groups**. A group is a set of things people can do, like "Maintenance" or "Tenant". Put someone in a group and they can do what it allows. <!-- for: landlord -->

### The short version
- **Everyone is in one or more groups.** Each group says what its people can do.
- **More groups = more they can do.** Groups only ever *add*. Nothing in one group takes away what another gives.
- **Admins can do everything**, whatever their groups.
- **Someone in no group can't see or do anything yet.** Home tells them to ask whoever added them.
- **Changes take effect right away.** No logging out needed. Their Home catches up the next time they open the app.

*Why it works this way:* it's how Linux does it, and it's the simplest system anyone's come up with. Because groups only add, you never have to figure out *why* someone can't do something. You just look at what their groups give them.

| **Operator** | Report a problem (a new work order) on the machines they're assigned to, and log readings. Sees those machines and every work order on them, plus the ones they reported. No parts. |

### The groups you start with
Every company starts with these three. Change them, rename them, or make your own.

| Group | Can |
|---|---|
| **Landlord** | Everything. |
| **Maintenance** | See the properties, and see, add and work every maintenance request (start, finish, add outside services). See what maintenance is due and say when it was done. |

### What people see
Two rows decide what someone sees at all: **Assets** and **Work Orders**.
- **Assets, View on**: every asset. **View off**: only the ones they're **assigned to**, or **added themselves**.
- **Work Orders, View on**: every work order. **View off**: the ones they **reported**, plus every one on the assets they're **assigned to**.

Everything else follows from that. A work order on an asset they can't see doesn't show up in search, in Home's counts or in Costs, and opening its link says **Not found**.

*Why:* an operator should see their own truck's problems without reading the whole shop's. A new hire who hops between machines? Give their group View on Assets. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* a tenant sees their own apartment's requests, never the neighbor's. <!-- for: landlord -->

### View, Add, Edit, Delete
Each thing in FleetOptima (assets, work orders, parts...) has four boxes, from least to most power:
- **View**: see the list and open things.
- **Add**: make new ones. With **Add without View** they still see what *they* added. That's how an Operator reports problems and checks on their own reports without seeing everyone else's.
- **Edit**: change them. For work orders, that includes starting and completing a job.
- **Delete**: remove them.

**On Assets and Work Orders, Edit and Delete work without View**, on the same ones they can see: someone with Edit on Work Orders but no View can start and finish the jobs on their own assets, and nothing else. The permissions page says so under those rows. **Everywhere else, Edit and Delete need View:** they stay greyed out until View is on, and turning View off clears them. *Why:* you can't fix what you can't see.

You only see the buttons you can use. Without Delete there's no Delete button; without Edit on work orders there's no Start or Complete.

### What each row covers
| Row | What it unlocks |
|---|---|
| **Assets** | The trucks and equipment, their history and maintenance. (See **What people see**.) | <!-- for: fleet -->

## Work orders

A work order is one job on one asset: "brakes squealing on Truck 12." <!-- for: fleet -->
A maintenance request is one problem at one property: "kitchen sink leaking in Apt 1A." <!-- for: landlord -->

### The list
**Home → Work Orders.** Jobs still to do (**Open jobs**) are on top: **urgent** ones nobody has started first, then the newest. Finished and cancelled ones are tucked into **History** at the bottom: tap it to slide it open.

Under the search box are **filters**: **All**, **Open** (open and in progress), **In progress**, **Done** and **Cancelled**. Tap one to see only those jobs, and **All** to see everything again. Tapping **open jobs** on Home opens the list with **Open** already picked.

### Reporting a problem
**Home → New Work Order** (under Quick actions). Pick the asset, give the job a **Title** ("Oil change", "Brakes squealing"), say **What's wrong**, and pick **How urgent?** (below). Notes go in the job's **Details** as you work on it. <!-- for: fleet -->
**Home → New Maintenance Request.** Pick the property (you only see yours), give it a **Title** ("Sink leaking", "No heat"), say **What's wrong**, and pick **How urgent?** (below). <!-- for: landlord -->

As you type the **Title**, the app suggests names your company already used ("oil" → **Oil change**). Tap one to use it, or keep typing something new. *Why:* when every oil change is called "Oil change," FleetOptima can tell they're the same job, which is how maintenance knows a job was done. You can also start from the asset itself: open the asset and tap **Create Work Order** (the asset is already filled in). <!-- for: fleet -->
As you type the **Title**, the app suggests titles already used on your requests. Tap one to use it, or keep typing something new. <!-- for: landlord -->

### Urgent
**How urgent?** is **Normal** or **Urgent**. <!-- for: fleet -->
**How urgent?** is **Normal** or **Urgent** ("It can't wait: no heat, no water, or a big leak"). <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Picking Urgent shows a red note: gas leak, fire, flooding, or someone hurt? Call 911 (or your gas or power company) first.** A maintenance request isn't an emergency line. <!-- for: landlord -->
- An urgent job nobody has started has a red **URGENT** chip, sits at the top of the list, and is counted first on Home ("1 urgent").
- Once someone starts it, it goes back to its normal place in line: it's being handled.
- **Mark urgent / Not urgent** in the job's **⋯** menu (for people who work jobs). The job's history says who changed it.
- Email alerts can be set to **Only urgent ones** (see **Email alerts**), and urgent emails say **Urgent** in the subject.
- **On the job's page**, an urgent job that isn't finished yet has a **red outline** around its name and a red **URGENT** tag, even after someone starts it. It goes back to normal when it's done or someone marks it **Not urgent**.

### Where a job is (its status)
| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Reported, nobody's working on it yet |
| **In progress** | Someone tapped **Start** |
| **Done** | Completed |
| **Cancelled** | Stopped without finishing |

**Where the buttons are:** **Complete** is the big button at the bottom of the job, always under your thumb (an Open job has **Start** next to it). The **⋯** at the end of the job's status row has **Complete**, **Cancel job**, **Mark urgent** and **Delete**. **Edit** is on the job itself.

- **Start** moves an Open job to In progress.
- **Complete** works from Open *or* In progress. If you open and finish a job in one go, you don't have to tap Start first.
- Completing asks for the asset's **reading now** (miles or hours), filled in with the last one: leave it if it hasn't changed, or type the new one. Assets with no meter just ask **Finish this job?** <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Cancel job** (under the **⋯**) works from Open or In progress.
- Status can only change through these buttons, not by editing the job, so every change is recorded.
- A done or cancelled job can't be started, completed or cancelled again, so those buttons go away.

### Outside services on a job
When someone outside your company does the work, log it on the job: **Services → Add service**.
- **Who did it** ("Joe's Plumbing"), **what they did**, and **what it cost**.
- **Don't have the receipt yet?** Leave the cost blank. The service says **cost needed**, and Costs lists it under **Missing a cost: find the receipt**. Tap **Set cost** when it turns up.
- **Already used them before?** Start typing and the names you've used come up ("d" offers **Dave's Plumbing**). Tap it instead of typing it again.
- **The same name typed differently is one vendor**: "Dave's Plumbing", "daves plumbing" and "DAVE'S PLUMBING." all become the first spelling you used, so Costs doesn't split one plumber into three.
- **Edit** (on the service) fixes the name, what they did, or the cost. The job's history keeps the old values.
- **Remove** takes it off if it was a mistake. It comes out of Costs, and the job's history keeps a note of it.
- A job can be finished with only services and no parts.

*Why:* the tire shop, the tow, the plumber: real money spent on your things, even though nobody pulled a part off your shelf. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* most of what a building costs is people you call: the plumber, the electrician, the HVAC company. <!-- for: landlord -->

Turn this off if nobody outside ever works on your things: **Settings for your whole company → Service tracking**.

### Parts on a job
Only if your company keeps parts (**Parts inventory** in **Settings for your whole company**).
- **Pull part** opens a search. Type a name, a part number or a barcode, or tap the **scan** button and point the camera at the box. Pick the part, then say how many.
- **Not in the system yet?** Type its name and tap **Add "..." as a new part** (or scan a box nobody has scanned before). Fill in what it's measured in, how many you have and what each cost, and you're straight back to pulling it. You never leave the job.
- Parts leave stock **right away**, so two people can't grab the same last filter. If there isn't enough on the shelf, the app tells you how many are there.
- The **Parts** section shows how much of each part the job has and what it cost, plus the **parts total** for the job. Measured parts say their unit (**4 qt Castrol 5W-30**); parts counted one by one say **2 x FRAM PH3614**.
- **Return** puts unused parts back on the shelf, and the job's cost goes down by exactly what they were charged.
- **Forgot to log a part before finishing?** Pull part and Return still work on **completed and cancelled jobs**. The job stays finished, and its records say the change was **added after the job was completed**, so nobody mistakes it for something that happened during the job. Nothing that's already there gets edited.

### Cancelling a job
Tap the **⋯** next to the job's status, then **Cancel job**. When you cancel a job that still has parts out, the app lists them under **Back on shelf**, all ticked, because a cancelled job usually didn't happen. **Untick anything that got used up** (oil already poured in, a part that turned out to be defective); it stays charged to the job. A cancelled job can still have a cost.

### Details (a job's notes and history)
Every work order keeps a list of what happened, oldest first: reported, started, parts pulled, services added, urgent or not, edited, completed or cancelled, plus any notes.
- Each record shows **who** did it and when.
- **Add note** adds your own note (just type it).
- **Details can't be deleted or edited.** If something was wrong, add a note that corrects it. That keeps the history trustworthy.
- Deleting a work order hides it, but its records and its costs are kept.

## Maintenance

A maintenance rule is a job that comes around on a schedule: "**Oil change**, every **5,000 miles** or **6 months**, whichever comes first." FleetOptima keeps count for every asset the rule covers and makes the work order when it's due. <!-- for: fleet -->
A maintenance rule is something that needs doing on a schedule: "**Change the AC filter**, every **3 months**." FleetOptima keeps count for every property the rule covers and makes the maintenance request when it's due. <!-- for: landlord -->

### Adding a rule
**An asset → Maintenance → + Add rule.**
- **Job**: what the work order will be called ("Oil change"). <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Job**: what the request will be called ("Change the AC filter", "Test the smoke detectors"). <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Every how many** miles or hours (the asset's own unit), and/or **every** so many days, weeks, months or years. Fill in one or both. With both, **whichever comes first** wins: a timing belt at 5 years or 70,000 miles is due at 5 years even if the truck only did 20,000. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Every** so many days, weeks, months or years. <!-- for: landlord -->
- An asset with no meter (a place, a trailer) only asks for the time. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Apply to**: who gets it, with how many each choice covers right now: **All Trucks (12)**, **All Ford F-150 Trucks (6)**, **Just Truck 12**. It starts on the narrowest group, not the whole fleet. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Apply to**: **Just Apt 1A**, or **All Properties** (with how many that covers right now). It starts on just this one. <!-- for: landlord -->

You never build groups by hand: FleetOptima works out which assets match from what's on them (type, make, model, year). Add a new F-150 later and it **joins the rule on its own**. <!-- for: fleet -->
Add a property later and an **All Properties** rule picks it up **on its own**. <!-- for: landlord -->

*Why:* 10 trucks shouldn't mean typing the same oil change 10 times. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* 20 apartments shouldn't mean typing the same filter change 20 times. <!-- for: landlord -->

**Turned a rule off and back on?** It starts counting again from that day, marked "assumed", so the time it was off doesn't make a job overdue. If the job was really done some other time, set when it was last done.

### "Counting from today"
When you add a rule, FleetOptima doesn't know when it was last done on each one, so it **starts counting from today** and tells you which ones. Their maintenance shows **(assumed)** until someone fixes it.

Know the real date? **The asset → Maintenance → Done when?** Set the day it was done (and the reading, for things with a meter). "(assumed)" goes away and the count starts from there. If that makes it overdue, the work order shows up right away.

*Why assume instead of asking for every one:* you can start using it right now and fix the dates as you find them, instead of digging up a pile of receipts before anything works.

### Coming up, due, and the work order
Each rule on an asset shows **Next** (when it's due), **Last** (when it was done), and where it stands:

| Shows | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OK** | Not close yet. |
| **Coming up** | In the last **20%** of the interval: about 5 weeks before a 6-month one. |
| **Due** | Time's up. A work order has been made. |
| **Skipped** | Its work order was cancelled; waiting for the next round. |
| **Off** | Turned off for this asset. |

**When it's due, the work order makes itself**, straight onto the list, called by the job's name, with "Auto-generated by maintenance rule" in its details. Nobody approves it first. There's only ever **one at a time**: until that job is finished, the rule doesn't make another. If you get **New Work Order** emails, you get one for these too.

Miles and hours are checked the moment someone logs a reading. Dates are checked **every morning at 7 AM Central** (8 AM Eastern), and whenever someone opens an asset's Maintenance. *Why "coming up" first:* so a due date is never a surprise. <!-- for: fleet -->
Dates are checked **every morning at 7 AM Central** (8 AM Eastern), and whenever someone opens a property's Maintenance. *Why "coming up" first:* so a due date is never a surprise. <!-- for: landlord -->
*Why the morning:* a work order (and its email) made at midnight just sits there or wakes someone up. At 7 it's waiting when the day starts.

### When the job's done
**Complete** the work order. The rule starts counting again **from when it was actually done**: due at 3,000 miles, done at 4,000, next one at 7,000. <!-- for: fleet -->
**Complete** the request. The rule starts counting again **from when it was actually done**. <!-- for: landlord -->

- **Did it before the rule asked?** A job with the **same name** on that asset counts too. Another reason to use the suggested job names.
- **Done by someone outside?** Make a work order for it, add the **service**, and complete it, so the history and the cost stay in one place. Or use **Done when?** on the asset.
- **Cancelled the work order?** That round is skipped: no new one until the next due point. (Want it gone for good? Turn it off, below.)

### Changing a rule
**The asset → Maintenance → Change.** Pick who the change is for:
- **All on this rule**: every asset follows the new interval. Assets with their **own** interval are **left alone** (the app names them).
- **Just this asset**: it gets its **own** interval ("Truck 7 is old: every 3,000"). Later changes for everyone don't touch it. <!-- for: fleet -->
- **Just this asset**: it gets its **own** interval ("Apt 1A has a cat: every month"). Later changes for everyone don't touch it. <!-- for: landlord -->
- **Follow the rule again**: drop its own interval.

*Why:* the fleet changes together (switched to synthetic oil), but the odd truck stays odd. <!-- for: fleet -->
*Why:* most places change together, but the odd one stays odd. <!-- for: landlord -->

### Turning it off
- **The asset → Maintenance → Turn off**: no more work orders from this rule **on this asset**. The rest keep it. **Turn on** brings it back.
- **On a work order a rule made → Stop this rule here** does the same, right where you noticed it.

*Why:* anything automatic should be easy to tell "no" at the moment it bugs you.

<!-- for: fleet -->
### Units
A rule counts in **one unit**: a rule in miles only covers assets measured in miles. A truck in kilometers needs its own rule. *Why:* "5,000" can't mean miles on one truck and kilometers on the next.

## Parts and stock
Only if your company keeps parts: **Parts inventory** in **Settings for your whole company**.

### Quantities
Quantities can be decimals: **4.5 quarts** of oil is fine. Each part has a **measuring unit** (quarts, units, gallons...). A part must have one, and if it's missing the app tells you to pick one.

### Barcode
Optional. It's the code on the box (UPC). Some shops use it as their part number, so when you search by code, FleetOptima checks **both** the barcode and the part number.

If one barcode matches more than one part, the app asks **Which one?** and lists them.

**Scanning:** the first time you use the scan button, your browser asks to use the camera. Say yes (you won't be asked again on that site). Scan a box once and the part remembers its barcode; after that, a scan finds it straight away.

The scan button is in these places:
- **Pull part** on a job: a known box goes straight to "how many"; a new one starts a new part.
- **The Parts list** (barcode icon, top right): a known box opens that part; a new one opens **New Part** with the barcode filled in.
- **The Barcode field** on New Part / Edit: fills the field. If that barcode is already on another part, the app says so and offers to open that part instead, so you don't make a duplicate.
- **The VIN field** on a new asset (Serial Number on equipment): scan the barcode on the **VIN sticker**. The app finds the VIN inside it (it knows the extra "I" some stickers add, and the square Data Matrix codes on newer ones), and a good VIN fills in the vehicle details just like typing it (see *Assets*). If what it read doesn't pass the VIN's check digit, it's still put in the field, with a warning to compare it to the sticker, and no details fill in. On equipment, a serial number tag is filled in exactly as scanned. <!-- for: fleet -->

### How stock is counted: oldest first
Every time stock comes in, it's kept as its own batch with **its own price**. When parts are used, the **oldest batch is used first** (first in, first out).

Why: prices change. If you bought 5 quarts at $6 and later 5 at $7, a job that uses 7 quarts is charged **(5 × $6) + (2 × $7) = $44**, which is what that oil actually cost. FleetOptima never averages prices.

### The buttons on a part
| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Restock** | Stock came in: how many, and the cost each. Creates a new batch at that price, which becomes the part's latest price. Don't know the cost? Leave it blank (see below). |
| **Count** | "The shelf says 4": enter what's actually there and stock changes to match. If the shelf has less, the missing amount comes off the oldest batches; if it has more, the extra is added at the latest price. |
| **Use or toss** | Stock leaving **without** a work order. A **reason is required**: used outside a work order, defective and thrown out, returned to the store, lost, or other. |

**Stock never leaves silently.** Every change either belongs to a work order or has a reason.

You can't change the quantity by editing the part. Use Restock, Count or Use or toss.

### Reading it off a photo (no barcode)
Some fields have a **camera** button: **VIN** (Serial Number on equipment), **mileage / hours**, and **part number**. Use it when there's no barcode, like a VIN plate behind the windshield, your dashboard, or a label on the old part. <!-- for: fleet -->
The **part number** field has a **camera** button. Use it when there's no barcode, like a label on the old part. <!-- for: landlord -->
1. Tap the camera. Use **1x / 2x / 3x** to zoom in until the number fills the screen (what you see is what gets read), then take the photo.
2. The photo comes back with a **box around every bit of text** the app found, each with a little **tag showing what it read**. Compare the tag with the real thing underneath.
3. The app's best guess is **green, thick, with a check mark**. Everything else is red. Tap a different box (or its name in the list below) if the guess is wrong.
4. If one character is off, fix it in the box at the bottom. Then tap **Use ...**.

**VINs get extra checking.** Worn plates get misread in predictable ways (a 6 read as a 5, an 8 as a B), and a VIN can never contain I, O or Q. If what was read fails the check digit, the app tries those common mix-ups, keeps only the fixes that pass, and asks NHTSA which ones are real vehicles. The guess says what it changed ("Fixed 5 → 6 at position 6") so you can check it against the plate. If more than one fix is real, there's no guess: you pick. If NHTSA can't be reached, the check-digit guess stands and says it wasn't checked. <!-- for: fleet -->

**Mileage / hours** only picks whole numbers of 3 digits or more (with a decimal for hour meters), so the speedometer's "60" or a trip meter label won't be guessed. <!-- for: fleet -->

Nothing saves until you tap Save on the form, and the photo itself isn't kept. Tips: hold steady and avoid glare. You can hold your phone upright or turned on its side, whichever fits what you're reading.

### When nobody knows what it cost
A jug that was already on the shelf, a box from who-knows-when: leave **Cost each** blank instead of guessing. The stock is saved and usable, but marked **Cost needed**: on the part's Stock list, on its History, and on any job that used it ("+ cost needed on some" after the parts total).

When you find the receipt, open the part and tap **Set cost** on that batch. The cost fills in everywhere at once, including the jobs that already used it. A cost can only be filled in once like this. A cost that was already known never changes, so job totals stay trustworthy.

Why not just put $0? Because $0 looks like a real price, and the owner's numbers would quietly be wrong. A blank that says "cost needed" is honest.

### Stock and History
- **Stock** lists the batches that still have something left, oldest first, with what each one cost.
- **History** lists every change to the part's stock, newest first: restocks, pulls for work orders, returns, counts and adjustments, with their reasons.
